## Why I built it

I do not have a coding background. What I do have is a long dislike of repetitive work, and a clear view of how good marketing gets made: research, writing, critique, production, review.

In 2026, I asked a simple question: could AI do that work the way a well-run department does, with roles, handovers and a manager, instead of one chatbot doing everything?

## How the team works

**Four members, each with a role.** Claude, GPT, Gemini and an open-source runner each take the work they do best. Every task gets the right model, not the most powerful one: lighter models for routine work, the strongest models only where quality matters.

**One shared memory.** The whole team reads from the same knowledge base, so context is never lost between tasks or members.

**A manager, and a human with the final say.** An orchestrator plans the work, calls the members and writes the result back. I approve key decisions from my phone, in a simple web app where every project looks like a group chat.

**Built-in critique.** For a script, all four members write their own version. I choose one, the other three critique it, and the author finalizes it. That beats any single model working alone.

## What it produces

- **Topic research**, collected continuously into a topic bank for my channels.
- **Scripts and posts**, written, critiqued and finalized by the team.
- **Finished videos in one click**: once I approve a script, the team creates the voice-over and the edited video by itself.
- **Websites and tools**, built with AI from design files.

## Results

| Area | Result |
|---|---|
| Running cost | **about USD 85 per month** for the whole team, on a self-managed server |
| Cost per explainer video | **from ~VND 650,000 to a few thousand dong**, previously a writer, a voice talent and manual editing |
| My role | **deciding what to make and judging quality**, not doing the repetitive steps |

## What I learned

Not everything should be automated. When I let the members coordinate freely on a coding project, the process became too complex, so I went back to a simpler, more manual flow for that work. Knowing where to stop automating is part of the design.

**AI does not replace judgment. It multiplies the value of good judgment, and the cost of bad judgment.**

The same principle drives my work with businesses: I apply AI where it changes results, such as the AI-powered lead system behind [10+ weekly site visits at Tâm Phát Real](/case-studies/tam-phat-real/).
