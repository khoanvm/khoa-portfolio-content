Scroll through property ads in Ho Chi Minh City and you will see the same lead form again and again.

*Are you interested in a studio, one bedroom or two bedrooms? Name. Phone number.*

It looks simple. It is not.

Every project has different sizes and prices for each apartment type. Someone who has just seen an ad does not know them yet. So the very first question asks for a decision they are not ready to make.

Worse, it ignores the biggest difference between buyers: some want a home, others want an investment. They need completely different conversations.

The common belief is that shorter forms convert better. That is only half true. **People do not abandon forms because of the number of questions. They abandon them because of the difficulty of the questions.**

I had learned this years earlier at audibene in Berlin, where the order of a questionnaire changed how many people finished it ([read that story](/stories/designing-a-questionnaire-around-progressive-commitment/)). So I rebuilt the form as a conversation:

1. **Are you looking to invest or to live?** Easy, and it tells us which conversation to have.
2. **Have you bought property before?** So the salesperson knows how much to explain.
3. **What matters most to you?** Location and quality of life, early entry and payment terms, or return on investment. The customer names their own goal.
4. **Would you like to visit the sales gallery and model apartment?** A small commitment, and a reminder that a visit is possible.
5. **Where should we send you information?** iMessage, Zalo, WhatsApp or Viber. Choosing the channel is a quiet promise to pick up when we call.

Each answer makes the next one easier. Behavioral scientists call it the foot-in-the-door effect, and consistency does the rest.

Together with many small ad tests, the new form helped **triple monthly leads while cutting cost per lead by about 75%**, and it gave sales a better first sentence for every call.

**The lesson:** A form is not a database. It is the first conversation a customer has with your company.
