At first, using AI for content and video work meant switching between different tools.

One tool could transcribe audio. Another could propose a storyboard. Another could generate visuals. A video editor could assemble everything.

Each tool was useful, but I was still responsible for connecting the steps, transferring context, correcting outputs, and checking the final result.

That led me to a different question.

What if I designed the workflow first, then used AI and software to support it?

I began developing Khoa Video Studio and Khoa Content Studio around tasks I understood from my own work.

The process brought new challenges: product requirements, model selection, version control, tests, permissions, costs, and the question of when human judgment must remain in control.

The products are still evolving.

But building them has changed how I approach AI adoption.

**The lesson:** Access to powerful AI is becoming easier.

The harder skill is understanding a problem deeply enough to build a reliable process around it.
